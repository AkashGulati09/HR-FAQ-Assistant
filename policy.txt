## Annual Leave

Full-time employees receive 12 days of paid annual/earned leave per calendar year. Leave should be requested at least 3 working days in advance through the HR portal, except in genuine emergencies. All leave requires manager approval.

## Casual Leave

Employees receive 7 days of casual leave per year for short-notice personal matters. Casual leave may be requested on the same day by informing the manager. No more than 3 consecutive casual leave days may be taken.

## Sick Leave

Employees receive 7 days of paid sick/medical leave per year. A doctor's note is required for sick leave exceeding 2 consecutive days. No more than 5 consecutive sick-leave days may be taken without a formal medical certificate.

## Monthly Leave Entitlement

There is no separate fixed monthly leave quota. Leave is provided as annual allowances: 12 annual leave days, 7 casual leave days, and 7 sick leave days per calendar year. The applicable approval and consecutive-leave rules still apply.

## Continuous Leave

Employees cannot take more than 10 consecutive working days of leave in a year , combining any leave type, without additional HR approval. Requests exceeding this limit must be submitted at least 2 weeks in advance and approved by both the manager and HR.

## Half-Day and Short Leave

A half-day leave deducts 0.5 days from the annual leave balance and must be requested through the HR portal. Short leave of less than 3 hours only requires manager notification.

## Leave Carry-Forward and Encashment

Up to 5 unused annual leave days may be carried forward into the next calendar year. Remaining unused days expire on December 31. Leave is not encashed except as part of final settlement.

## Work From Home

Employees may work from home for up to 2 days per week with manager approval. WFH requests should normally be submitted at least 1 day in advance through the HR portal.

## Maternity and Paternity Leave

Maternity leave is 26 weeks of fully paid leave. Paternity leave is 10 working days of fully paid leave and should be taken within 3 months of the child's birth.

## Expense Reimbursement

Work-related expenses up to Rs. 5,000 per claim may be submitted through the finance portal with a valid receipt. Reimbursement is processed within 10 working days of manager approval.

## Resignation and Notice Period

The standard notice period is 30 days for confirmed employees and 15 days during probation. The notice period may be bought out with written manager and HR approval.

## IT Equipment and Company Assets

Employees are issued a company laptop and basic accessories where required. Damage or loss must be reported to IT within 24 hours. Company equipment must be returned in working condition when employment ends.

## Working Hours and Attendance

Employees are expected to follow their assigned working schedule and maintain regular attendance. Employees should inform their manager as early as reasonably possible if they expect to be late or absent. Repeated unexplained absence may be reviewed by the manager and HR.

## Monthly Attendance and Payroll

Attendance is reviewed at the end of each month for payroll processing. Employees should ensure that their attendance and approved leave records are accurate before the monthly payroll cutoff. Salary is processed once per month according to the company's payroll schedule. Payroll discrepancies should be reported to HR within 5 working days of receiving salary.

## Workplace Conduct

Employees are expected to maintain professional and respectful conduct toward colleagues, managers, customers, and business partners. Harassment, discrimination, threats, deliberate misconduct, or misuse of company resources may be referred to HR for review.

## Performance Improvement Plan [role: manager, hr]

Managers may initiate a formal Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) for an underperforming employee. HR must be consulted before the PIP is finalized and communicated to the employee.

A PIP typically runs for 30–60 days with documented weekly check-ins and must be logged in the HR system before being communicated to the employee.

## Monthly Team Check-In [role: manager, hr]

Managers should conduct a regular monthly check-in with team members to discuss workload, priorities, performance, and blockers. Significant performance or attendance concerns should be documented and discussed with HR when appropriate.

## Team Attendance and Leave Approval [role: manager, hr]

Managers are responsible for reviewing leave and WFH requests, maintaining reasonable team coverage, and addressing attendance concerns. Repeated or serious attendance issues should be escalated to HR.

## Salary Band and Compensation Reviews [role: manager, hr]

Compensation reviews take place annually in April. Managers may recommend salary changes within their team's approved budget band. Any recommendation above the approved band requires HR and department-head approval before being communicated to the employee.

## Grievance and HR Escalation [role: hr]

HR is responsible for reviewing formal employee grievances, workplace complaints, and escalated policy disputes. HR may request relevant documentation before making a decision.

## Termination and Disciplinary Procedures [role: hr]

All termination decisions must be reviewed and approved by HR before being communicated to the employee. Disciplinary actions normally follow a documented three-step process: verbal warning, written warning, and final review. Immediate termination without this process requires director-level approval.

## Policy Interpretation [role: hr]

HR is the final point of contact for questions involving policy interpretation, exceptions, or conflicts between policies. Where a situation is not covered by this handbook, employees and managers should contact HR rather than assume an exception applies.
